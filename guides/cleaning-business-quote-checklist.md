# Cleaning business quote checklist: scope, time and job costs

A free, copyable review for independent cleaning-business owners. Use it before sending a quote, then compare the estimate with what you record after the job. You can use the checklist without buying software or providing an email.

This is a planning aid, not a recommended rate, contract, tax calculation or guarantee of earnings. Use fictional or redacted information here; keep customer addresses, access codes and private records out of public GitHub issues.

## Before you send the quote

| Check | Your notes |
| --- | --- |
| Visit type and condition | Is this a regular visit, first clean or a different agreed scope? What condition assumptions still need checking? |
| Included work | Which rooms, tasks and supplies are included? What does completion mean for this visit? |
| Exclusions | Which tasks are excluded, such as oven interiors or exterior windows? Avoid assuming the customer knows. |
| Time | Record your own expected working time and any relevant travel/setup time once. Record helper hours separately. |
| Costs | Enter your chosen value for owner time, employee costs, supplies, travel cash costs and allocated overhead. Avoid counting the same cost twice. |
| Fees | Record your actual provider's percentage and flat fees if applicable; do not assume a sample fee is your own rate. |
| Approval and changes | Who agrees the scope? How will added rooms, extra tasks or delays be discussed before you proceed? |
| Price and timing | Record your own agreed price, any applicable taxes separately, due date, payment method and visit window. |
| Uncertainty | What changes if the work takes one additional hour? What facts should you check before committing? |

## A fictional extra-hour example

These figures are for arithmetic only, not market rates.

- Three owner hours at a chosen $20 per hour: $60.
- Supplies $10, travel cash costs $10, allocated overhead $20.
- Total modeled cost: $100.
- Quoted service price: $133.33, before tax.
- Example percentage fee: 5% of the quoted price, rounded to $6.67.
- Residual after these entered costs and fee: $133.33 - $100 - $6.67 = **$26.66**.
- If owner time becomes four hours at the same entered value, modeled cost becomes $120. With the same price and fee, the residual becomes **$6.66**.

One extra hour changes the result by $20 in this example. Small rounding differences arise when using an unrounded quote. The residual is after the entered owner-time allowance; it is not a promise of take-home pay. Taxes, flat fees and omitted costs can change the result.

Before changing a future quote, record why the time differed: condition, extra tasks, access delays or the original estimate. A single example does not establish what you should charge.

## Five-minute post-job review

1. Record the agreed service price and any approved additions.
2. Record actual owner/helper hours, supplies and travel.
3. Check the actual payment-provider fees.
4. Compare estimated and recorded costs using the same categories.
5. Note one scope or estimating assumption to check next time.

Copy this blank record into your own notes:

```text
Visit reference (no sensitive customer details):
Agreed included tasks:
Excluded tasks:
Condition/access assumptions to check:
Estimated owner hours / recorded owner hours:
Estimated helper hours / recorded helper hours:
Owner-time value you chose:
Supplies / travel / allocated overhead:
Estimated fee / actual fee:
Agreed price / approved additions:
Who approved scope or changes:
Difference to investigate before the next quote:
```
